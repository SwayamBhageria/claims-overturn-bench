# Findings

Five, in the order they matter. Every figure here is regenerated by `python -m bench.run`,
`python -m bench.rates` and `python -m tools.findings`; none is typed by hand. The corpus is
1,533 published Financial Ombudsman decisions, 2024-01-08 to 2026-07-21, across 183
respondent firms, of which 1,349 are about a claim decision.

## F1 — When a declined claim reaches a final decision, the insurer loses about four times in ten

7,701 of 18,736 published decisions mentioning a declined claim were upheld: **41.1%** (95%
CI 40.4%–41.8%). That comes from the search's own result totals — two requests, with none of
our sampling in it.

It is a rate over *published decisions*, which are the tail of complaints that reached an
ombudsman at all. It is not a claims error rate, and nothing here estimates one.

## F2 — Roughly three in ten of those losses never touch the claim decision

**28.6%** of upheld claim complaints leave the claim outcome intact (95% CI 21.7%–36.5%,
from 140 decisions read by hand). The declinature stood. The insurer lost on delay, on
evidence it never asked for, on an explanation the customer could not follow.

The rule that reproduces this across the whole corpus puts it at 31.8%, inside the
hand-labelled interval. Measured on a sample drawn and labelled *after* the rule was frozen:
accuracy 85%, precision 90.3%, recall 90.3%.

**An earlier version of that rule said half.** It counted "accept the claim and repair the
laptop" and "increase the cash settlement to £355" as the claim being left alone. That is
why 140 decisions were read by hand rather than left to a regex.

## F3 — The complaint costs more than the compensation does

Median compensation, where any is awarded, is **£200** (422 of 626 upheld decisions, 67%).
The ombudsman's case fee is **£680**, and its own wording is the part worth quoting:
*"Respondent financial businesses pay a case fee regardless of the outcome of a complaint."*

The fee for the complaint existing is 3.4 times the median award for losing it. The claim
itself, 8% interest, handling time and the client conversation sit on top; only the fee and
the award are measured here.

**That £200 is the median across all upheld decisions and is not the figure for F2's
subset.** Where the claim decision stood, the median award is **£250** (151 of 199), and the
fee is 2.7 times it rather than 3.4. Those cases are also the ones where the award is the
whole bill, because no claim is paid on top. F2 and F3 sit next to each other and measure
different sets; quoting one median against the other's population is the easiest mistake to
make here.

## F4 — The overturned decisions are identifiable before they are sent

On the facts alone, under a split where no insurer appears on both sides:

| | balanced accuracy | recall on upheld |
|---|---:|---:|
| majority predictor | 0.500 | 0.000 |
| TF-IDF + logistic regression | 0.687 | 0.649 |
| nearest published precedents | 0.666 | 0.660 |

Paired McNemar against the majority predictor: p = 2.7e-17.

Grouped and random splits agree to within 0.1 points, so this is not the model recognising
the insurer. Nor is it a product on its own: the triage curve in §4 of the README is the
honest version, and it says a system like this earns its place by escalating rather than by
deciding.

## F5 — Showing a model the prior adjudicator's opinion is worth 8 points

1,294 of the cases carry the first-stage adjudicator's provisional view. With it removed the
linear model scores 0.681; with it restored, 0.761.

That is the finding worth taking somewhere else. **Any evaluation of a claims model run over
files that already contain a human's opinion is overstating it by roughly this much**, and a
production file at the moment of decision does not contain one.

Making it measurable took three separate leak fixes: the adjudicator's recommendation
surviving a line-based stripper, plural "our Investigators" never matching, and decisions
restating a provisional decision's own intended outcome. Before those, the headline model
scored 0.746 rather than 0.687 — and nothing failed.
